# Search Intent and Keyword Research Protocol

## Important limitation

All keywords in data/master-600.csv are **hypotheses** extracted from proposed article titles. No volume, difficulty, CPC, SERP position or trend is claimed. These must be measured with available tools and representative regional searches.

## Before writing a P0 article

1. Verify primary query formulation and related questions in English or Bangla.
2. Identify the dominant SERP intent: explainer, procedure, comparison, pricing, product navigation, or mixed.
3. Identify whether Google currently prefers documentation, videos, forum threads, tools or long-form editorial pages.
4. Record competing pages and last-reviewed date; use primary documentation to validate product claims.
5. Compare with NIMORAI's nearest existing article to prevent cannibalization.
6. Select one primary query and a short semantic family; do not force exact-match repetitions.
7. Score the topic's product relevance, original-evidence feasibility and realistic review capacity.
8. Rewrite title/description and outline after research; record decision in article brief.
9. Write a direct answer, then supporting explanation, original material, caveats and contextual CTA.
10. Verify intent after publication using Search Console queries. Merge or redirect overlapping content where warranted.

## Query families

- Definition: What is X? What does X mean?
- Process: How to do X? What happens after X?
- Comparison: X vs Y; which tool fits a given workflow?
- Cost: What does X include? Why do fees differ?
- Trust: How do I verify X? Is this transaction or delivery confirmed?
- Local: Bengali vocabulary and regional educational question patterns.
- Branded: NIMORAI feature documentation after deployment proof.

## Research log fields to add before a post is ready

Research date, target country/language, query intent, SERP top sources, verified search volume if available, tool/source and sampling window, ranking competitiveness observations, evidence gap, planned originality asset, canonical destination, reviewer.

Do not label a topic "low competition" or "high volume" without evidence.
